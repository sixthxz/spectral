Neutral summary: the chain begins with Euler’s additive reciprocal-power series, then compresses integer factorization into Euler’s prime product. Dirichlet generalizes the same architecture into coefficient-weighted Dirichlet series and (L)-functions. Riemann turns the half-plane object into a meromorphic function of a complex variable, then normalizes it by gamma and powers of (\pi) so that the functional equation becomes the reflection (s\leftrightarrow1-s). The xi function removes the pole and packages the relevant zero problem into an entire symmetric function. The zeros then split into trivial zeros and nontrivial zeros; the latter are confined to the critical strip, with the critical line as the fixed line of the reflection. Hadamard’s product makes the completed function a product over its zeros, and the 1896 PNT proofs use zero-free information on (\Re(s)=1), not RH, to obtain the first-order prime asymptotic. Later spectral language re-encodes the same critical-line condition as a hypothetical real-spectrum statement, but it is not a completed formal transformation or a proof.
